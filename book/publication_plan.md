# Publication Plan

## Purpose
Define the editorial process and delivery discipline for a book written through **symbiotic human computation**.

This plan governs how lived episodes, formal concepts, and system governance converge into a publication-ready manuscript without drift.

## Scope
Applies to the full manuscript from Prologue through Epilogue.

This plan assumes the book is already **concept-complete** and focuses on:
- Editorial convergence
- Structural tightening
- Contract enforcement
- Execution discipline

## Current State
You already have:
- A closed and defensible conceptual system (geometry, engines, gears, Digital Māyā, Ma’at)
- A governed narrative architecture (spine, emotional arc, red lines)
- Two anchor chapters (Prologue and Chapter 1)
- Canonical concepts formalized and enforced
- A working symbiotic human–AI writing loop

You do not need:
- More ideas, chapters, or concepts
- More metaphors
- More personal history

You do need:
- Cohesion enforcement across chapters
- Redundancy pruning
- Concept compression
- Editorial finish passes

## Governance Check
Before any editorial work proceeds, confirm these governance documents remain authoritative and unchanged:
1. Book Spine - narrative contracts and red lines.
2. Concept and Vocabulary Canon - definitions, terms, and reuse phrases.
3. Emotional Arc Map - grief limits, agency return points, and tonal guidance.

## Three Editorial Passes (System-Aware)

### Pass 1 — Structural Integrity (No Prose)
- Confirm every chapter serves the spine contract.
- Ensure each chapter explicitly interrogates at least one canonical engine.
- Verify that every death referenced is traceable to engine failure.
- Identify sections to cut, merge, or relocate.
- Do not perform line-level edits.

### Pass 2 — Concept Compression (Ledger-Guided)
- Collapse repeated explanations into canonical phrases.
- Replace restatement with callbacks to earlier assets.
- Ensure concepts appear only where they add new consequence.
- Prune metaphor drift.
- Use the asset ledger as the authority for reuse.

### Pass 3 — Language and Music (Late-Stage)
- Tighten sentences in high-intensity sections.
- Reserve long sentences for reflection or synthesis.
- Remove grant-style or defensive phrasing.
- Preserve authorial inevitability.

## Reader Target
Pick one ideal reader: a technically literate, morally serious lay reader.

The book is not written for:
- specialists seeking novelty
- activists seeking affirmation
- skeptics seeking debate

This choice governs pacing, depth, and explanation.

## Lock the Ending
Freeze the emotional destination now: agency reclaimed. Every late chapter must move toward custody, participation, governance, and dividends returned. The ending must demonstrate custody returned through enforceable systems, not rhetoric.

## External Reality Check
Before querying:
- Share Part I + Prologue with one clinician and one non-medical reader.
- Ask only: "At what point did the system stop making sense, or death stop being legible as a systems outcome?"

## Do Not Do
- Do not rewrite Prologue or Chapter 1.
- Do not add more autobiography.
- Do not add more citations to prove authority.
- Do not explain AI or racism basics.
- Do not weaken governance language for comfort or marketability.
