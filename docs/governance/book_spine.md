# Dividends & Deaths - Book Spine (Narrative Contracts)

## One-Sentence Thesis
Modern precision medicine and AI are extraction economies that convert human life into codes, capture the dividends upstream, and leave the deaths downstream; this book argues that the future depends on returning custody, provenance, and economic participation to the patients whose bodies power the system.

## What This Book Is
- A narrative-economic anatomy of how medicine, genomics, and AI monetize human life
- A first-person systems account from inside clinical medicine, genomics, and AI
- A moral and technical argument for patient-owned provenance and medical dividends
- A blueprint for redesigning precision medicine using existing legal and technical tools

## What This Book Is Not
- Not a memoir for its own sake
- Not an anti-technology screed
- Not a generic DEI critique
- Not a futurist fantasy disconnected from law and infrastructure
- Not a book about "AI replacing doctors"

## Core Throughline (Must Hold in Every Part)
Translation -> Extraction -> Asymmetry -> Digital Maya -> Custody -> Reassignment

1. Human reality is translated into standardized symbols (codes)
2. Those symbols enable extraction at scale
3. Extraction concentrates dividends and distributes deaths
4. The system begins acting on the abstraction (Digital Maya), not the person
5. The root problem is custody of that abstraction
6. The solution is reassignment, not abolition

## PART-BY-PART CONTRACTS

### PROLOGUE - Moral Origin
Reader must feel: Shocked, implicated, oriented
- Henrietta Lacks establishes extraction without consent
- Dividends vs deaths introduced as twin currencies
- Emotional authority is earned here; it will not be re-litigated later

### PART I - The Economy We Inherited
Reader must understand: This system was designed, not accidental

- Chapter 1: The Billable Code
  - Establishes the billable code as the atomic unit of value
  - Shows how legibility replaces personhood and introduces the Digital Maya lens

- Chapter 2: Dividends
  - Maps where value flows (pharma, AI, genomics, hospitals)

- Chapter 3: Deaths
  - Proves asymmetry using breast cancer mortality and global data

Exit belief: The system is coherent, profitable, and morally inverted

### PART II - Extraction: From HeLa to 23&Me
Reader must see: This pattern repeats across eras

- Henrietta is not an exception; she is the prototype
- 23&Me is not a failure; it is a business success
- Underrepresentation is structural, not incidental

Exit belief: Good intentions do not stop extraction

### PART III - The Circular Economy of Medicine and AI
Reader must realize: AI did not invent this - it automated it

- Medicine built the first circular data economy
- AI scales it to trillion-dollar loops
- The legal asymmetry between medicine and AI is the twist

Exit belief: Regulation already exists - just not where value moved

### PART IV - Rewriting the Economy
Reader must feel: Agency returning

- Provenance is the missing primitive
- mCODE is grammar, not software
- OPTS-EGO is infrastructure, not ideology

Exit belief: This is technically and legally possible

### PART V - A Patient-Centered Future
Reader must envision: Participation instead of extraction

- Medical dividends are plausible
- Mortality is the only KPI that matters
- Community is infrastructure, not support

Exit belief: Equity can be operationalized

### PART VI - The Future We Deserve
Reader must commit: This future is a choice

- Social contract for precision medicine and AI
- 2035 vision is concrete, not speculative

Exit belief: Inaction is also a decision

## Red Lines (Non-Negotiables)
- Henrietta Lacks is not re-explained after Part II
- Codes are never portrayed as neutral
- AI is never anthropomorphized
- Equity is defined in outcomes, not intentions
- Mortality gaps are the ultimate metric

## Ending Contract
The book must end with custody returned - not optimism, not outrage, but reassignment of power to the people whose lives are represented by the system.
