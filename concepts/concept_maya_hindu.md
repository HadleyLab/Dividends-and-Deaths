# Māyā (Hindu Concept)

## Purpose
Define Māyā as a philosophical anchor for representation and illusion.

## Scope
Applies to passages naming the Hindu concept and its framing in the book.

## Category
Lens

## Canonical Definition
Māyā is the Hindu concept of appearance mistaken for reality.  
In this book, it frames how representations can eclipse the lives they claim to depict.

## Usage / Placement Rules
- Use only when explicitly invoking the philosophical concept.
- Distinguish from **Digital Māyā**, which is the system-level manifestation.
- Do not frame as spirituality or mysticism; keep it structural.

## Relationship to Canonical Concepts
- **Digital Māyā:** the institutionalized form of representational illusion.
- **Ma’at:** the counterforce that binds representation to accountability.
- **Legibility vs Personhood:** the conflict that Māyā makes visible.

## Reusable Phrase (Canonical)
“Māyā names the illusion of representation mistaken for reality.”

## Death-Bound Clarification
When systems treat representation as reality, they act on shadows.  
Those shadows misdirect care and can become fatal.
