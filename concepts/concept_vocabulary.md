# Dividends & Deaths — Concept & Vocabulary Canon

## Purpose
Define non-negotiable meanings, terms, and phrases used throughout the book.

## Scope
Applies to all chapters and protocols.

## Category
Vocabulary

## Canonical Definition
This file is the authoritative vocabulary for the book’s core concepts, terms, and phrases. It constrains meaning so the manuscript can scale without drift.

### Core Economic Concepts (Canonical Definitions)

#### Billable Code
- The standardized symbolic representation that converts human care into economic value.
- Not neutral; it is the atomic unit of the precision medicine economy.
- Capitalize when referring to the abstraction (Billable Code); lowercase when colloquial.

#### Precision Medicine Economy
- The multi-trillion-dollar system built on extracting value from coded biological and clinical data.
- Includes genomics, pharma, imaging, AI, EHRs, insurers, and data brokers.
- Measured in dividends and deaths, not innovation metrics.

#### Three Engines of the Precision Medicine Economy
- The coupled engines that convert human life into value: discovery, care, and information.
- Failure in any engine produces downstream harm; failure across all three produces structural mortality.
- Use once as explicit framework, then reuse as upstream/downstream language.
- These are the only canonical engines; other candidates are gears inside the three.

#### Medical Dividend
- The economic value generated from patient data to which patients currently have no claim.
- A future construct: royalties, equity, shared savings, or participation rights.
- Always framed as structural, never charitable.

#### Extraction
- The conversion of human life into symbols for profit without consent, custody, or compensation.
- Distinguished from research, which can be ethical only if governance exists.

### Representation and Power

#### Custody
- Who controls, governs, and can act on a representation.
- The central failure of modern healthcare and AI is misassigned custody.

#### Provenance
- The traceable lineage of data from human origin to downstream use.
- Includes consent, transformation, reuse, and value generation.
- Treated as a right, not a feature.

#### Legibility
- The condition of being representable to a system.
- Legibility enables action but can erase context.
- Never equated with personhood.

### Technical and Infrastructure Terms

#### mCODE
- A grammar for oncology data, not a software product.
- Enables portability, transparency, and patient agency.
- Always described as foundational infrastructure.

#### OPTS-EGO Ledger
- **OPTS:** Open Provenance Token Standard — ensures traceable data lineage.
- **EGO:** Ethical Governance Operators — validate consent and integrity.
- Framed as trust infrastructure, not ideology or cryptocurrency.

#### Healthcare Data Laws
- U.S. policy decisions that shaped how health data is collected, controlled, and monetized.
- Use for chapters on data rights, provenance, or governance; keep framing structural, not partisan.
- Anchor claims in specific laws (HIPAA, HITECH, 21st Century Cures Act).

#### Circular Data Economy
- A closed loop where data generates value that funds further extraction.
- First perfected in medicine; later automated by AI.

### Narrative and Philosophical Anchors

#### Dividends and Deaths
- The book’s central accounting framework.
- Dividends flow upward to institutions.
- Deaths fall downward on those excluded from datasets.
- Always paired; never referenced independently.

#### Digital Māyā
- Representation mistaken for reality: the institutional shadow of a person that systems act on first.
- Arises when symbols (codes, models, risk scores) detach from custodial responsibility.
- Digital Māyā is not error; it is structural illusion produced by scalable systems.
- Lives in Chapter 1 as a named concept; thereafter it returns as effect, not definition.
- Always invoked in relation to misaction, custody failure, or provenance loss.

#### Geometry of Medicine
- The representational regime where people become vectors, illness becomes position in a learned space, and care becomes boundary decisions.
- The math-native sibling of Digital Māyā: the system's shape, not the illusion itself.
- When the geometry is wrong or incomplete, dividends rise and deaths fall.

#### Ma’at
- The principle of order, balance, truth, and rightful accounting.
- Represents alignment between representation and reality, action and consequence, value and responsibility.
- In this book, Ma’at names the governance logic required to constrain power.
- Where Ma’at is absent, imbalance becomes institutionalized.
- Where Ma’at is violated, systems appear functional while producing harm.
- Never framed as spirituality or mysticism; always as structural accountability.

#### Māyā–Ma’at Asymmetry
- Modern systems are optimized for Māyā (scalable representation) without Ma’at (accountable order).
- This asymmetry produces profit without responsibility and scale without justice.
- The precision medicine economy is a Māyā-maximized, Ma’at-deficient system.
- Restoring Ma’at requires custody reassignment, not better models.

#### Community as Infrastructure
- Patient communities are active discovery engines, not support groups.
- Infrastructure implies durability, governance, and agency.

## Usage / Placement Rules
Reference this file whenever a core term appears in prose or a ledger.

### Language Rules (Non-Negotiable)
- Do not synonym-swap core terms.
- Avoid passive constructions when describing harm.
- Do not anthropomorphize AI.
- Avoid moralizing language; describe incentives instead.
- Equity is defined by outcomes, not intentions.
- Character assets must include Cultural/National Identity in ledgers; avoid parenthetical identity tags in prose.

### Phrases to Reuse Verbatim
- “The billable code is the fundamental economic unit of American healthcare.”
- “Dividends rise. Deaths fall.”
- “Codes are not neutral.”
- “Custody, not capability, is the core problem.”
- “The precision medicine economy runs on three engines--discovery, care, and information. When any one fails, harm follows. When all three fail together, deaths are inevitable.”
- “Modern medicine is geometry: people become coordinates, care becomes boundaries, and death becomes the only metric that proves whether the space was built truthfully.”
- “Mortality is the only KPI that matters.”
- “Where Māyā scales without Ma’at, harm becomes invisible.”
- “Governance is Ma’at rendered operational.”

### Red Lines
- No redefining of Henrietta Lacks after Part II
- No framing extraction as accidental
- No solution without governance
- No optimism without custody reassignment
- No invocation of Māyā without structural consequences
- No invocation of Ma’at without governance implications

- **Three Engines (Discovery / Care / Information):** fixes engine names and failure phrasing so ledger mapping stays stable.
- **Geometry of Medicine:** locks the geometric framing and its canonical reuse lines.
- **Digital Māyā:** preserves the definition and mandated reuse language.
- **Ma’at:** fixes governance language and prevents drift into moralizing.

## Death-Bound Clarification
When vocabulary drifts, mechanisms become ambiguous and harm loses traceability. A misnamed system failure becomes a misframed death; precision of language is therefore a safety constraint, not a stylistic preference.
