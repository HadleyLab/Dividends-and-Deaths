# Mortality Gap

## Purpose
Define the measurable difference in death outcomes across groups.

## Scope
Applies to passages that quantify inequity in survival.

## Category
Policy Context

## Canonical Definition
The mortality gap is the measurable difference in death outcomes between populations that share incidence but not representation, access, or custody.

## Usage / Placement Rules
- Use when comparing outcomes across groups with similar disease burden.
- Tie any gap to engine failure and representation deficits.

## Relationship to Canonical Concepts
- **Mortality as Ledger:** the gap is the ledger’s imbalance.
- **Underrepresentation in Trials:** gaps widen when discovery excludes.
- **Billable Code:** gaps persist when care follows incomplete records.

## Reusable Phrase (Canonical)
“Mortality gaps are the system’s audit trail.”

## Death-Bound Clarification
A mortality gap is not a statistical curiosity; it is structural harm made legible.  
If the gap persists, governance has failed.
