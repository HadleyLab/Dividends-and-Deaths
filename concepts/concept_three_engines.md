# Three Engines

## Purpose
Define the structural engines through which the precision medicine economy generates value and produces harm when governance fails.

## Scope
Applies to chapters that describe how discovery, care, and information systems turn representations into decisions, value, and mortality.

## Category
System Primitive

## Canonical Definition
The precision medicine economy operates through three coupled engines:
1. Discovery
2. Care
3. Information

Each engine converts human life into value. Each engine can fail independently. Each engine, when misgoverned, produces deaths downstream while dividends flow upstream.

The engines are sequential but reinforcing. Failure in any one is sufficient to produce harm; failure across all three produces structural mortality.

These are the only canonical engines. Any additional candidates are gears within Discovery, Care, or Information, not separate engines.

### Engine I -- Discovery

#### Definition
Discovery converts biological material and clinical data into knowledge: targets, biomarkers, models, trials, and intellectual property.

#### Inputs
- biospecimens (tumors, blood, tissue)
- genomes and molecular profiles
- clinical outcomes
- patient participation (often uncompensated)

#### Outputs
- drug targets
- diagnostic criteria
- risk scores
- clinical trial protocols
- patents and proprietary datasets

#### Failure Mode -> Death
Exclusion from discovery produces invisibility.

When populations are underrepresented or misrepresented:
- diseases are mischaracterized
- therapies are optimized for others
- risk models fail silently

Deaths emerge years later, labeled as "biological disparity" rather than representational failure.

#### Canonical Rule
Discovery failure does not look like error. It looks like absence.

### Engine II -- Care

#### Definition
Care converts discovered knowledge into billable clinical action. It is where representation becomes intervention.

#### Inputs
- guidelines
- risk scores
- diagnostic categories
- formularies
- prior authorizations
- clinical decision support

#### Outputs
- treatments delivered or withheld
- procedures approved or denied
- time gained or lost

#### Failure Mode -> Death
Misrepresentation in care produces misaction.

When:
- codes outrank bodies
- risk scores outrank lived context
- guidelines outrank judgment

Care becomes mechanically precise and clinically wrong.

Deaths appear as:
- delayed diagnosis
- undertreatment
- overtreatment
- "noncompliance"

#### Canonical Rule
In care, the system acts first on the abstraction, and only later—if ever—on the person.

### Engine III -- Information

#### Definition
Information governs how representations move, persist, and compound across systems. It determines custody.

#### Inputs
- electronic health records
- claims data
- imaging archives
- genomic databases
- AI training sets

#### Outputs
- portability or lock-in
- auditability or opacity
- reuse or erasure
- profit or accountability

#### Failure Mode -> Death
Loss of custody produces uncorrectable harm.

When patients cannot:
- access
- correct
- revoke
- audit
- participate

Errors compound invisibly across time and institutions. Deaths surface far downstream, disconnected from the original misrepresentation.

#### Canonical Rule
Information failure is not data loss. It is custody loss.

### Coupled Failure: How Deaths Are Manufactured
The engines do not fail independently in practice. They form a loop:

Discovery -> Care -> Information -> Discovery

When governance is absent:
- discovery excludes
- care misacts
- information locks errors in place
- discovery trains on the damage

This is the circular death economy underlying precision medicine. Dividends rise at every pass. Deaths accumulate silently.

### Non-Canonical Gears (Overlays That Bias Engines Toward Death)
These are **not** additional engines. They are **gears** that constrain, steer, or intensify the three canonical engines.

#### Incentive Gear
- **Couples to:** Discovery, Care
- **How it biases toward death:** decides what is worth discovering and what is worth treating; profitable pathways scale first

#### Procurement Gear
- **Couples to:** Care
- **How it biases toward death:** contracts, formularies, and vendor lock-in narrow the action space before clinicians act

#### Trust Gear
- **Couples to:** Discovery, Information
- **How it biases toward death:** determines whose data is believed and who participates; distrust produces representational absence

#### Labor Gear
- **Couples to:** Care
- **How it biases toward death:** time scarcity and workload compress judgment into protocol and throughput; nuance is priced out

#### Liability Gear
- **Couples to:** Care, Information
- **How it biases toward death:** defensive thresholds and documentation logics prioritize institutional risk minimization over patient harm minimization

#### Attention Gear
- **Couples to:** Care
- **How it biases toward death:** scarcity of bandwidth creates invisible triage; what is not attended to is not treated

#### Canonical Framing Line
“Engines produce action. Gears decide who that action serves.”

## Usage / Placement Rules
- Introduce the engines explicitly once (Part I or early Part III).
- Thereafter, refer to them implicitly:
  - "upstream discovery failure"
  - "downstream care misaction"
  - "information lock-in"
- Never anthropomorphize the engines.
- Never frame failure as accidental.

## Relationship to Canonical Concepts
- **Digital Māyā:** emerges when the engines act on representations without custodial responsibility.
- **Ma’at:** names the missing constraint that should bind discovery, care, and information to consequence.
- **Provenance:** the mechanism that reconnects the engines to human origin.
- **Custody:** determines whether the engines generate dividends alone—or dividends and survival.

## Reusable Phrase (Canonical)
“The precision medicine economy runs on three engines—discovery, care, and information. When any one fails, harm follows. When all three fail together, deaths are inevitable.”

“Everything else is a gear: incentives, procurement, trust, labor, liability, attention—forces that torque the engines toward profit or toward death.”

## Death-Bound Clarification
An **engine** is canonical only if its failure can **directly contribute to patient death** and simultaneously **fuel the precision medicine economy**.

A **gear** is a non-canonical overlay that **biases** one or more engines toward profit or toward death, but does not itself produce medical action/inaction or durable representational compounding.

The precision medicine economy has **exactly three engines**: **Discovery**, **Care**, and **Information**.
Everything else is a **gear** that torques these engines.

### Engine Separation Test
A proposed “engine” must **own exactly one** of these concerns:
1. **Create** new medical knowledge (Discovery work)
2. **Act** on a patient (Care work)
3. **Persist/transfer/compound** representations (Information work)

If it overlaps multiple concerns, it is a **gear** or a **system**, not an engine.

### System Mortality Test
Structural mortality emerges when the **three engines are coupled** and governance fails:
- Discovery excludes
- Care misacts
- Information locks errors in place

### What Actually Kills Patients (Structural)
Patients die when one (or more) of the following occurs:
- **Never discovered:** exclusion or misrepresentation upstream makes disease invisible
- **Acted on incorrectly:** delays, denials, misapplied guidelines, wrong thresholds
- **Uncorrectable error:** no access, correction, audit, revocation, or provenance

These map directly to the canonical engines: **Discovery**, **Care**, **Information**.

### Structural Claim (Book-Level)
Mortality is not the cost of innovation. It is the output of misgoverned engines.

If a patient dies, at least one canonical engine failed—and someone benefited.
