# Multiplicative Risk

## Purpose
Define risk that compounds through interaction rather than addition.

## Scope
Applies to passages describing overlapping conditions that amplify harm.

## Category
Lens

## Canonical Definition
Multiplicative risk is the compounding of risk factors such that combined exposure produces outsized harm, not a simple sum.

## Usage / Placement Rules
- Use when describing compounding outcomes across disease, environment, and access.
- Do not treat as a statistical flourish; connect to lived consequences.

## Relationship to Canonical Concepts
- **Comorbidity:** interacting conditions drive compounding risk.
- **Social Determinants of Health:** context multiplies clinical risk.
- **Mortality Gap:** compounded risk widens survival inequity.

## Reusable Phrase (Canonical)
“Risk multiplies when conditions interact.”

## Death-Bound Clarification
When multiplicative risk is modeled as additive, severity is underestimated.  
That underestimation leads to delayed care and preventable death.
