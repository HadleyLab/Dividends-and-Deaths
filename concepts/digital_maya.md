# Digital Maya

## Purpose
Keep Digital Maya present without repeating its definition.

## Scope
Applies to all chapters after the initial definition in Chapter 1.

## Usage
Treat Digital Maya as a lens, not a slogan.

- Name Digital Maya once in Chapter 1.
- Thereafter, let readers recognize it through effect, not exposition.
- If you must name it again, do so sparingly (mid-book nod, pre-epilogue callback).

---

## Death-Bound Clarification

Digital Māyā is not an abstraction error. It becomes lethal only when it is allowed to **govern action without custody**.

Digital Māyā contributes to patient death when:
- Representations outrank bodies in **care decisions**
- Model outputs are treated as truth without **audit or appeal**
- Errors persist because no actor holds **custodial responsibility** for correction

In this book, Digital Māyā is therefore not a cause of death by itself.

It is the **operating condition** that allows failures in **Discovery, Care, and Information** to persist without consequence.

## Chapter-Level Direction

### Chapters 2–3 (Translation → Extraction)
- Do not name Digital Maya.
- Show how codes travel farther than patients and how systems act on abstractions instead of bodies.
- Let readers feel the illusion without labeling it.
- Show how early representational shortcuts quietly pre-decide later mortality.

### Mid-Book (Chapters 6–10)
- Gesture back with phrases like "the version the system sees," "the institutional mirror," "the shadow self that makes decisions first."
- One line such as "By now, you can probably name this illusion for yourself" is enough.

### Later Chapters (Provenance, OPTS-EGO, Dividends)
- Focus on custody: who holds the abstraction, who can revoke it, who benefits when it is wrong.
- You are no longer explaining what Maya is; you are litigating who owns it.
- Structural evolution: Illusion → Extraction → Custody → Contract.
- Make explicit that restoring custody is the only way to constrain Digital Māyā without eliminating representation itself.

## When to Name It Again

1. **Pre-Epilogue:** Single recognition line (e.g., "In older traditions this would have been called Maya—not because it is false, but because we mistake it for the whole.")
2. **Final sentence:** Tie custody to Digital Maya ("The task ahead is not to destroy the illusion, but to return it.")

No additional definitions.

## Stylistic Guardrails

- Never use it as a buzzword or product pitch.
- Avoid over-spiritualizing; keep it grounded in systems and epistemology.
- Always connect it to consequences (custody, misaction, provenance).
- Make readers feel like they already knew the concept.

## Canonical Relationship to Engines and Geometry

- **Three Engines:** Digital Māyā is the condition under which Discovery, Care, and Information act on representations instead of people.
- **Geometry of Medicine:** Digital Māyā names the moment the system mistakes position in space for personhood.
- **Ma’at:** Governance is the constraint that binds Digital Māyā to consequence.

Digital Māyā explains **how drift happens**.

Engines explain **where death is instantiated**.

Geometry explains **why that death becomes mathematically inevitable**.

None of these are interchangeable.
