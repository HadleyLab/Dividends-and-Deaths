# Redlining

## Purpose
Define redlining as structural denial encoded in geography and policy.

## Scope
Applies to passages describing disinvestment, segregation, and unequal access.

## Category
Policy Context

## Canonical Definition
Redlining is the historical and ongoing practice of denying investment and services to communities based on race and geography.
Its effects persist in health infrastructure, access, and outcomes.

## Usage / Placement Rules
- Use when describing spatially encoded exclusion in care.
- Tie to downstream access and mortality patterns.

## Relationship to Canonical Concepts
- **Access:** redlining blocks entry into care.
- **Coverage:** coverage gaps map onto redlined geographies.
- **Mortality Gap:** geographic exclusion becomes measurable mortality.

## Reusable Phrase (Canonical)
“Redlining is policy encoded in geography.”

## Death-Bound Clarification
Redlining leaves communities without timely care and infrastructure.  
The resulting delays and deficits produce preventable death.
