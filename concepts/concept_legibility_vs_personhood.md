# Legibility vs Personhood

## Purpose
Define the conflict between system representation and lived identity.

## Scope
Applies to chapters describing how systems act on records instead of people.

## Category
Lens

## Canonical Definition
Legibility vs personhood is the tension between what a system can represent and what a person actually is.
Systems act on legibility; people live in personhood.

## Usage / Placement Rules
- Use when describing how records overrule lived context.
- Tie the tension to concrete system consequences.

## Relationship to Canonical Concepts
- **Legibility:** the representational side of the conflict.
- **Digital Māyā:** legibility replaces personhood at scale.
- **Billable Code:** the mechanism that turns personhood into legibility.

## Reusable Phrase (Canonical)
“Legibility is not personhood.”

## Death-Bound Clarification
When systems mistake legibility for personhood, care follows the record, not the body.  
That misalignment produces preventable harm and death.
