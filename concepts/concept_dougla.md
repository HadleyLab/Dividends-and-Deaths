# Dougla

## Purpose
Define Dougla as a contextual identity construct in the book.

## Scope
Applies to passages describing mixed Afro‑Indo Caribbean lineage and social classification.

## Category
Policy Context

## Canonical Definition
Dougla is a Caribbean term for mixed African and Indian ancestry, used here as a lived social category rather than a biological taxonomy.

## Usage / Placement Rules
- Use only in contexts of cultural translation and lived classification.
- Do not reduce the term to genetics; it is a social and historical construct.

## Relationship to Canonical Concepts
- **Legibility vs Personhood:** identity labels simplify lived complexity.
- **Colonialism:** the category exists within colonial sorting systems.
- **Universal Men:** the term anchors mixed lineage in the narrative.

## Reusable Phrase (Canonical)
“Dougla is a social category, not a genetic equation.”

## Death-Bound Clarification
When identity is flattened into rigid categories, care pathways follow stereotypes instead of need.  
That misclassification compounds into delayed or inappropriate care.
